# Voice and content

Brilliant sounds like a sharp, kind tutor: confident about hard ideas, never cute, never condescending.

## Principles

- **Talk to one person as "you".** Short sentences. Plain words. Active voice.
- **Lead with the need, not the definition.** "Your app pays per token. What exactly is a token?" beats "A token is…".
- **Show before telling.** Put something to move or try before the explanation.
- **Titles state the idea or the finding.** "Unigram beats BPE at every size", "Three numbers shape every parabola" — not "Results", "Overview".
- **Numbers as numerals with units and context.** "2.8× fewer tokens than GPT-4o", "10 million+ learners".
- **Sentence case** for everything, including buttons and titles.

## Microcopy

| Moment | Copy |
|---|---|
| Primary actions | **Continue**, **Check**, **Start**, **Try again**, **Finish**, **Get started**, **Subscribe now** |
| Secondary | **Why?**, **Skip**, **Reset**, **Show me**, **Go deeper** |
| Correct | "Correct!" + one sentence of why (the only exclamation mark allowed) |
| Incorrect | "Not quite" + a hint that points at what to look at; never the answer, never blame |
| Retry | "Almost. Try again." |
| Empty state | What to do next, with one button: "No lessons yet. Start with Logic." |
| Errors | What happened and how to fix it: "We couldn't save your progress. Check your connection and try again." |
| Streaks | "3-day streak", "Finish a lesson to start a streak." |
| Upsell | Plain value, no pressure: "Unlock every course", "Premium gives you unlimited access to every course and more." |

## Marketing

- Hero: a promise in ≤ 8 words ("Your personal tutor for math and coding"), one supporting sentence, two audience CTAs.
- Proof: big numbers with tiny labels ("100,000+ / 5-star app store reviews").
- Feature rows: a 4–7 word title + 2–3 short paragraphs about what the learner does, not what the product is.
- Testimonials: real voice, first person, one or two sentences, attributed by role.

## Don't

- No emoji in UI, documents or decks.
- No exclamation marks except "Correct!".
- No jargon without a reason; define a term the first time with a `.term` marker.
- No "simply", "just", "easy", "obviously".
- No fake urgency, no dark patterns on paywalls.
